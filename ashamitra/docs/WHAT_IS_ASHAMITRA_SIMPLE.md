# What is ASHAMitra? — In Simple Words

*A plain-language handout: who the ASHA is, what she does, the problems she faces, how ASHAMitra helps, every feature, and the effect on mother & baby health.*

---

## 👩 Who is an ASHA?
**ASHA** = the **village health helper** — a trained local woman who is the first health worker for about **1,000 villagers**. Mothers, babies, and children are her main responsibility. She is the **bridge between the village and the hospital**.

## 📱 What is ASHAMitra?
A **mobile app that becomes her digital assistant.** It replaces her big pile of paper notebooks with one simple app on her own phone — and it quietly watches for danger so she never misses a sick mother or baby.

---

## 📒 What does an ASHA do all day? (her tasks)
- Visit **pregnant women** and check they are safe.
- Visit **newborns and small children**, look for danger signs.
- Make sure **vaccines** happen on time.
- Track **growth** (weight, malnutrition).
- **Refer** sick people to the hospital.
- Fill **15+ paper registers** and write a **monthly report** by hand.

## 😓 Her big problems
1. **Too much paperwork** — she writes the same details again and again in many notebooks.
2. **"Double-double" work** — she writes during the visit, then copies it all again at night.
3. **Danger gets missed** — a warning sign sits hidden in a notebook for weeks, until "reporting day."
4. **No follow-up** — she refers a mother to the hospital… and never finds out if she went.

## ✅ How ASHAMitra makes her work easy
- She **speaks in Bengali**, the app writes it down — visit notes drop from **~30 minutes to ~5 minutes.**
- The **monthly report writes itself** from the visits she already did (no more 2 nights of copying).
- The app **checks for danger automatically** and tells her clearly: 🟢 fine · 🟡 see a clinic · 🔴 emergency, refer now.
- It **remembers every due date** for her, so nothing is forgotten.

## 🧰 The features (in easy words)

| Feature | What it does (simple) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **🪪 Aadhaar QR scan** | She points the camera at the Aadhaar card; the app fills in name, age, gender, address by itself. | No typing long names by hand — and fewer spelling mistakes. |
| **🎙️ Bengali voice** | She talks to the app in her own language; it understands and can reply by voice. And the **whole app uses the everyday words she actually says** — BP, vaccine, checkup, "Overdue" — not textbook Bengali. | Even if typing is hard, she can just *speak* — and every screen reads the way she talks. |
| **🩺 Danger-sign check (triage)** | Asks a few simple questions, checks vitals, and gives a colour: green / yellow / red. | A newborn with fever instantly shows 🔴 "refer to SNCU now." |
| **📈 Child growth check** | It compares the child's **weight to the WHO standard** for that exact age and warns if the child is **underweight/severely underweight** or **not gaining weight** since the last visit; it also checks the right **development milestones** for the age. | A 9-month-old below the safe weight line shows a warning → refer for nutrition care. |
| **📅 Auto schedule** | From the pregnancy date (LMP) or baby's birth date, it builds all the visit dates — ANC check-ups, vaccines, newborn (HBNC) and child (HBYC) visits. | She never has to calculate "when is the next visit." |
| **🔔 Reminders by SMS + WhatsApp** | Before each due date, the app **messages the mother** automatically. | A mother gets a WhatsApp/SMS: *"Your ANC check-up is due in 3 days."* |
| **📄 Auto monthly report (HMIS)** | The government monthly report builds itself from her visits; she just reviews it. | 2 evenings of copying → a 10-minute check. |
| **🩷 Mother & Child card (PDF)** | One tap fills and prints the real government **Mother & Child Protection (MCP) card** — the family page, the **ANC check-up grid**, and the full **vaccine schedule with the date each dose was given** — showing only the pages that fit the case (mother or child). | The same card as the paper booklet, already filled in from her visits. |
| **🏥 Referral + map** | When she refers someone, it suggests the right hospital, shows **road directions**, and later tracks the **outcome**. | She finally learns: did the mother reach the hospital or not? |
| **📵 Works offline** | The danger-check works **without internet**, in a hut with no signal. | No network? Triage still works. |
| **🔴 Smart alerts** | A red (emergency) case alerts her and her supervisor instantly. | Danger is seen *today*, not on reporting day. |

## 🤰 How this helps mother & baby health
- **Danger caught early.** High-risk pregnancies are flagged **from the very first check-up** — not discovered too late.
- **The two biggest mother-killers watched closely:** **heavy bleeding after delivery (PPH)** and **fits from high blood pressure (eclampsia)** trigger an **immediate "refer now."**
- **Newborns protected.** *Any* fever, trouble feeding, or breathing problem in a baby = instant red flag → rushed to newborn care, where minutes matter.
- **Malnutrition caught at the village.** The arm-band (MUAC) **plus weight-for-age plus "not gaining since last time"** flag underweight and faltering children early — so nutrition help starts *before* it becomes severe.
- **No missed vaccines.** Daily reminders + a defaulter list mean fewer children fall through the cracks — and the child's whole vaccine card, with dates, prints in one tap.
- **Referrals actually complete.** Tracking shows *why* mothers don't reach hospitals (transport, cost, family) so the real problem can be fixed.
- **Reliable, on time, with proof** — the things that were *supposed* to happen on paper now actually happen, every time, recorded.

> **In one line:** *ASHAMitra turns the ASHA's pile of notebooks into a smart helper that does the writing, remembers every date, messages mothers, and never lets a danger sign go unseen — so more mothers and babies get help in time.*
